What will my wallet say?

You tapped a gift button, your wallet lit up with warnings, and you wondered if this is safe. Good instinct — here is exactly what it asks, what it can and can't do, and how to check it yourself.

The short version
  • Your wallet is asked to sign one message: “allow exactly the amount you chose in USDC to move once to osoartist.eth (0x25965899f4600b3AA5362ee4f34be95E5E62c934), valid for 5 minutes.”
  • It is not an approval or an allowance. Nothing is left open, nothing can be pulled later, and the message can be used only once.
  • You pay no network fee on Base — the payment service submits it for you. (That is why it is a signature and not a normal send.)
  • Wallets such as MetaMask often label this kind of gasless USDC payment a “high-risk approval” or say the recipient is an “untrusted EOA”. That is a generic caution for any such signature to an address the wallet hasn't seen before — other sites report the very same wording for ordinary x402 payments — not a verdict on this particular address. Your job is to check two things in the message: the amount and the address. If either doesn't match this page, tap reject.
  • Prefer not to sign anything? Send USDC on Base by hand — a normal send, a fraction of a cent of gas.

The screens you may see

  1. Connect — “shakaleikaumaka.com wants to see your account address.” It reads your public address only; it cannot move anything.
  2. Switch to Base — if your wallet is on another network it asks to switch to Base (chain 8453, Coinbase's network). Accept; the signature must be made on Base.
  3. Sign — a “Signature request” titled TransferWithAuthorization on USD Coin. This is where the red/yellow warning usually appears. Read the fields below, then approve or reject.
If a screen shows a full-page “This website might be harmful / Deceptive site ahead” warning before any of this, please don't force it — use the plain transfer below, and send us a screenshot at aloha@myagentohana.com. That is a wallet-side scanner opinion we want to correct with the wallet's security team.

Check the fields in your wallet's message

fromyour own address
to0x25965899f4600b3AA5362ee4f34be95E5E62c934 — osoartist.eth, the ʻohana's one gift address (see it on BaseScan ↗)
valueyour amount in millionths of a dollar: 1000000 = $1 · 10000000 = $10 · 100000000 = $100
validAfter0 (valid immediately)
validBeforeabout 5 minutes from now (a Unix time) — after that it is dead
noncea random one-time number — the same message can never be used twice
verifying contract0x833589fCD6eDb6E08f4c7C32D4f71b54bdA02913 — Circle's native USDC on Base
The exact message the page asks your wallet to sign 
{
  "types": { "TransferWithAuthorization": [
    {"name":"from","type":"address"}, {"name":"to","type":"address"},
    {"name":"value","type":"uint256"}, {"name":"validAfter","type":"uint256"},
    {"name":"validBefore","type":"uint256"}, {"name":"nonce","type":"bytes32"} ] },
  "primaryType": "TransferWithAuthorization",
  "domain": { "name":"USD Coin", "version":"2", "chainId":8453,
              "verifyingContract":"0x833589fCD6eDb6E08f4c7C32D4f71b54bdA02913" },
  "message": { "from":"<your address>", "to":"0x25965899f4600b3AA5362ee4f34be95E5E62c934",
               "value":"10000000", "validAfter":"0", "validBefore":"<now + 300 s>", "nonce":"<random 32 bytes>" }
}
This is the open EIP-3009 / x402 standard that Circle's USDC contract itself implements. The page code is open source and the signing is done by your wallet — we never see a key.

What this signature can and can't do

Can: move exactly the amount you chose from you to osoartist.eth, once, within 5 minutes.
Can't: move any other amount · go to any other address (the address is inside what you sign, and also fixed in the page and in our payment service) · be replayed (one-time nonce) · outlive 5 minutes · grant an allowance or approval · touch other tokens or other networks · reveal a seed phrase or key (we never ask for one).

If the payment service is down or something fails, the signed message is simply discarded and nothing leaves your wallet — the card says so in words. If it ever says “still settling”, the gift may still land; look at the address's token transfers ↗ a minute later.

Rather not sign? Give a plain way

  • Send USDC on Base by hand to 0x25965899f4600b3AA5362ee4f34be95E5E62c934 (osoartist.eth). Choose the Base network in your wallet and check the first/last characters. The card's “copy address” button gives you this exact address.
  • Card, PayPal or Venmo instead of a wallet: blessingpool.com lists the human routes.

Check us, don't trust us

  • Payment instructions, machine-readable: /x402-bless.json (payee, USDC, Base, facilitator).
  • The payment service (a small Cloudflare Worker, AGPL-3.0-or-later): gift-rail.opensourceorchestra.org/health · read its code — it pins the payee, the token, the network and a $0.001–$1000 range, then asks the PayAI facilitator to verify and settle.
